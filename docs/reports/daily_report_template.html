10Satellite Flood Detection & Impact

Each day the job runs a Sentinel-1 radar check at every watch location: stations rising or in flood, districts with 3 or more warning signs, and open flood incidents. Radar sees through cloud. Each check compares the latest pass with an earlier pass from the same orbit and removes permanent rivers and lakes, so only new water counts. When new flooding is found, the report estimates who and what is inside the flooded area.

10a. Satellite checks this period

LocationWhy checkedBefore passLatest passImage ageFlood extent fromNew flood areaResult
How simulated floods are tested: when a simulation run creates a flood, the job still downloads the real Sentinel-1 image for that location and draws the simulated flood extent on top of it. The impact counts then run on the simulated extent. This tests the full chain (imagery, exposure, impact, report) without a real flood. Simulated water is drawn hatched and real detected water is solid, so the two are never confused.

The satellite passes over each location about every 6 to 12 days, so the latest image can be several days old. The image age is always shown. A location with no recent pass is reported as such, not as "no flooding".

Sources & notes

River observations and forecasts come from the PMD Flood Forecasting Division (FFD). Modeled discharge comes from GloFAS and Google Flood Hub. Weather data comes from Open-Meteo. Satellite radar comes from Sentinel-1 RTC via Microsoft Planetary Computer, with Copernicus DEM elevation and JRC Global Surface Water used to exclude permanent water. Exposure uses gridded population data and OpenStreetMap. GloFAS gives modeled discharge, not measured water level. Travel times and "% of normal" are FloodGuard estimates. Each report keeps a copy of the data it used, so past reports can be reproduced.
